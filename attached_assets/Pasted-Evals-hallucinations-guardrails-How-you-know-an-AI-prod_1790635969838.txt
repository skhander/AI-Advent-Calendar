Evals, hallucinations & guardrails

How you know an AI product works — and where it can go wrong.

in plain English

AI can sound confident and still be wrong. Evals test whether a model behaves the way you expect. Hallucinations are answers that sound right but aren't supported by the facts. Guardrails are checks that help prevent or catch those failures.

testing an AI product

EVAL
“Is this answer good?”
Test against expected behavior

HALLUCINATION
“Is this answer true?”
Check against evidence

GUARDRAIL
“What happens if it goes wrong?”
Add checks, limits, and escalation

1 / under the hood

How it works

1

Test it
Give the model real, edge-case, and failure-prone examples.

2

Check it
Compare its answers against expected behavior or trusted sources.

3

Protect it
Add rules, validation, permissions, or human review where mistakes matter.

2 / outside the lab

Real-world use
Shopping assistant

Test whether recommendations match the shopper's request.

Customer support

Check that answers are grounded in your help center instead of made up.

Medical information

Require trusted sources and escalate questions the model shouldn't answer on its own.

3 / picture it

Think of it like this:

An AI product is like a new employee.

Before letting them work alone, you give them a set of tasks to see how they perform. You watch for mistakes, check their work, and put boundaries around anything they shouldn't handle themselves.

Evals are the test.
Hallucinations are the mistakes.
Guardrails are the boundaries.
